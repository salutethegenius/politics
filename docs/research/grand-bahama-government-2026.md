# Grand Bahama Government Structure (2026)

Reference doc: who holds what portfolio, who represents Grand Bahama (GB) in Parliament, and how local government works on the island. Compiled from official sources as of the May 2026 general election and the reappointed Davis Cabinet.

Sources are linked inline. Anything that could not be confirmed from a public source is flagged explicitly rather than guessed.

## 4. Grand Bahama Port Authority (GBPA) — parallel authority, not a ministry

GBPA administers the ~230 sq mi "Port Area" of Freeport under the 1955 Hawksbill Creek Agreement (HCA), a colonial-era agreement running through 2054. GBPA is responsible for infrastructure, municipal/community services, and development within the Port Area, and operates its own public data property, "Invest Grand Bahama."

**Recent and highly relevant development:** a February 27, 2026 arbitral tribunal ruling on a dispute between the Government and GBPA:
- Confirmed GBPA has an ongoing, enforceable obligation to make annual payments to the Government to defray administrative costs, through 2054.
- **Rejected GBPA's claim to exclusive administrative authority over Freeport and the Port Area**, affirming the Government retains continuing legislative and regulatory authority (licensing, immigration, customs, land acquisition, environmental regulation, development approvals, utility tariffs) even inside the Port Area.

This is a live, citable talking point: it strengthens the case that a *government-owned* GB data layer is politically timely, not redundant with GBPA's existing map. Sources: [OPM statement on the ruling](https://opm.gov.bs/government-of-the-bahamas-statement-on-the-historic-ruling-tribunal-confirms-the-grand-bahama-port-authority-must-make-annual-payments-to-the-government-and-people-of-the-bahamas-until-2054-tribuna/), [PM Davis's House of Assembly communication](https://opm.gov.bs/prime-minister-davis-hoa-communication-gbpa-arbitration/), [GBPA's own description of the Port Area](https://gbpa.com/freeport-city/).

## 5. Local government structure

Governed by the **Local Government Act, 2024** (in force December 2024, replacing the 1996 Act), overseen by the **Department of Local Government (DLG)**, which sits under Clay Sweeting's Ministry of Works and Family Island Affairs.

**National structure:** 33 Local Government Districts.

- **Second Schedule districts** (13, larger islands): two-tier system. Residents directly elect **Town Committees** for town areas; town-committee chairs (plus additional indirectly-elected members) sit on a 7–9 member **District Council**.
- **Third Schedule districts** (20): unitary. A single 5–9 member **District Council** is directly elected, with no town-committee layer. **The City of Freeport** is a Third Schedule district with an expanded, slightly different set of enumerated powers (it combines council and town-committee functions).
- Every council is a body corporate (can contract, sue/be sued, hold property) but needs ministerial approval to hire staff or dispose of real property.
- A **Family Island Administrator** (formerly "Island Commissioner") liaises between central government and each council.
- Elections occur every 3 years, first-past-the-post. Most recent: **January 23, 2025**. Next expected January 2028.

Source: [Department of Local Government — Key Structures](https://dlg.gov.bs/key-structures), [Local Government Act, 2024 (bill text)](https://laws.bahamas.gov.bs/cms/images/LEGISLATION/BILLS/2024/2024-0083/2024-0083.pdf), [Wikipedia — Local government in the Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_Bahamas).

**Grand Bahama's local-government districts:** Sweeting's Cay, East Grand Bahama, Central Grand Bahama, Pineridge, Marco City, West Grand Bahama, and the City of Freeport (per the [2025 election announcement](https://www.bahamas.gov.bs/index.php/news-press-release/residents-vote-for-their-candidate-in-local-government-election-2025) and the [2025 DLG training rollout coverage](https://znsbahamas.com/local-government-officials-receive-comprehensive-training-on-grand-bahama/)).

**Not publicly confirmable:** current named Chief Councillors for most of these districts. The only two names that surface in public reporting are Shervin Tate (Chief Councillor, Sweeting's Cay) and Mahalia Williams (Secretary, West Grand Bahama District Council). DLG's own site has no searchable roster, election-results archive, or per-district dashboard — results are reported only via one-off press releases (BIS, Tribune, ZNS), not structured public data. This is itself the gap worth building against.

## 6. Summary of the gap this points to

- GBPA has a public investment/data map ("Invest Grand Bahama"). The Ministry for Grand Bahama, whose own portfolio explicitly includes "Community Engagement and Empowerment" and "Promotion of Employment and Entrepreneurial Opportunities," has no equivalent.
- The Department of Local Government has no digital roster or dashboard of the ~7 GB districts, their councillors, election results, or town-committee structures — everything is scanned PDFs and press-release transcription.
- The February 2026 arbitration ruling gives the Government fresh, current-events grounding to justify building its own data infrastructure for Freeport/GB rather than relying on GBPA's.
